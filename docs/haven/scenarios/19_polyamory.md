### Scenario 1: The Closed Triad — First Living Together Negotiation
- **Context:** Three partners (A, B, and C) in a committed closed triad preparing to move into a shared three-bedroom home.
- **Dynamic:** Equilateral committed triad with shared domestic life.
- **What they want:** Establishing transparent agreements regarding sleep arrangements, finances, and private 1-on-1 time.
- **Before:** Facilitating a structured Haven Triad Summit over a weekend, mapping out room assignments and expectations.
- **Experience:** They agree that while each has their own personal bedroom for privacy, a large master bedroom serves as the shared retreat. A rotational schedule ensures that every dyad (A-B, B-C, A-C) gets dedicated 1-on-1 dates weekly.
- **Branches:** If two partners experience friction, the third partner does NOT act as an umpire; the dyad must resolve their conflict privately.
- **Aftercare:** Cooking their first family dinner in the new kitchen, toasting to their unique and beautiful life structure.
- **Reflection:** A triad is not one relationship of 3 people; it is 4 distinct relationships (A-B, B-C, A-C, and ABC together), each needing care.
- **Haven opportunities:** Tripartite agreement signing; multi-person calendar with dyad date filters; shared household expense tracker.

### Scenario 2: Metamour Coffee & First Meeting Alignment
- **Context:** Two men who are both in love with the same female hinge partner meeting for the first time.
- **Dynamic:** Kitchen-Table Polyamory (KTP) metamour friendship.
- **What they want:** Demystifying the 'other partner', replacing imaginary rivalry with human warmth, and establishing comfort boundaries.
- **Before:** The hinge partner stays home; the two metamours agree to meet at a neutral, quiet coffee shop for 45 minutes.
- **Experience:** They shake hands, order coffee, and talk about work, hobbies, and how much they both care for their shared partner. The awkwardness dissolves within 10 minutes as they realize they share similar values and respect.
- **Branches:** If one metamour prefers 'Parallel Polyamory' (minimal contact), they establish respectful communication parameters without forced friendship.
- **Aftercare:** Both text the hinge partner separately with warm updates, relieving her of intense anxiety.
- **Reflection:** Meeting metamours in person transforms an intimidating ghost into a real, likable human being.
- **Haven opportunities:** Metamour boundary profile settings (Parallel vs. Kitchen Table); communication preferences; hinge facilitation guide.

### Scenario 3: Holiday & Family Calendar Coordination in a Quad
- **Context:** Two couples who have formed an interconnected polyamorous quad balancing Thanksgiving with biological families.
- **Dynamic:** Multi-household holiday diplomacy.
- **What they want:** Ensuring everyone feels included, respected, and unpressured during complex family holiday seasons.
- **Before:** Setting up a shared holiday matrix on Haven 60 days before November.
- **Experience:** They designate Thanksgiving Day for biological extended family visits, and the following Saturday for their sacred 'Friendsgiving / Quad Holiday Feast' with all four partners and kids.
- **Branches:** If outside in-laws are hostile to polyamory, partners agree on protective discreet protocols when visiting non-accepting families.
- **Aftercare:** Returning to their private sanctuary, sharing leftover pie, and celebrating their collective harmony.
- **Reflection:** Creating alternative chosen-family traditions relieves the pressure to force acceptance from conservative relatives.
- **Haven opportunities:** Multi-partner holiday calendar sync; chosen-family tradition planner; privacy shield for conservative family events.

### Scenario 4: The Hinge Burnout & Boundary Reset
- **Context:** A hinge partner feeling overwhelmed trying to meet the emotional and logistical needs of two separate partners.
- **Dynamic:** Hinge emotional self-care and boundary re-anchoring.
- **What they want:** Stopping the emotional leakage where the hinge acts as an emotional sponge and messenger between partners.
- **Before:** The hinge calls for a 7-day 'Hinge Personal Sanctuary Week' where each partner gives them space.
- **Experience:** The hinge establishes firm boundaries: no more venting about Metamour A to Metamour B; no more managing each other's schedules. Each partner is empowered to take full responsibility for their own emotional regulation.
- **Branches:** If a partner pouts or demands special treatment, the hinge holds the boundary calmly without guilt.
- **Aftercare:** The hinge takes a solo weekend retreat at a cabin, resting, journaling, and regaining their sense of self.
- **Reflection:** A healthy hinge is not a servant to two masters, but a sovereign individual who loves two people freely.
- **Haven opportunities:** Hinge burnout assessment tool; emotional compartmentalization guide; solo sanctuary scheduling.

### Scenario 5: Polycule Emergency & Hospital Visitation Protocol
- **Context:** One partner in a polycule is involved in a serious motorcycle accident.
- **Dynamic:** Crisis management, legal rights, and multi-partner hospital presence.
- **What they want:** Ensuring all loving partners can visit and support without causing drama with hospital staff or conservative biological parents.
- **Before:** The injured partner had executed a Medical Healthcare Proxy and Haven Emergency Contact Directive naming their primary and secondary partners.
- **Experience:** With legal paperwork in hand, the partners rotate hospital shifts smoothly—one stays during daytime, another manages meals and communications, another stays overnight, coordinating like a synchronized team.
- **Branches:** When biological parents arrive, the polycule steps back graciously into a supportive background role to keep the ICU peaceful for the patient.
- **Aftercare:** Gathering at home for soup, debriefing the medical updates, and supporting each other through the scare.
- **Reflection:** True polyamory reveals its immense strength in crises: an expanded circle of loving hands to carry the burden.
- **Haven opportunities:** Medical healthcare proxy document vault; hospital visitation shift coordinator; emergency crisis team alert.

### Scenario 6: Navigating Disparity in Outside Dating Success
- **Context:** In a polyamorous couple, one partner has 5 active dates a week while the other has had zero matches in 2 months.
- **Dynamic:** Managing asymmetry, self-worth, and dating fatigue.
- **What they want:** Supporting the struggling partner without diminishing the successful partner's joy.
- **Before:** A dedicated evening to talk through the gendered realities of dating apps and feelings of rejection.
- **Experience:** The active partner intentionally dials back their dating frequency to spend more quality time at home, while helping the other revamp their profile photos and explore local community interest groups.
- **Branches:** The struggling partner focuses on personal hobbies, fitness, and friendships rather than measuring self-worth through app matches.
- **Aftercare:** A fun date night together, reaffirming that app algorithms do not reflect human desirability.
- **Reflection:** Asymmetry is inevitable in non-monogamy; compassion and pacing prevent resentment from festering.
- **Haven opportunities:** Dating asymmetry reflection guide; profile review and optimization checklist; self-worth off-app prompts.

### Scenario 7: Polyamorous Breakup & Metamour Community Containment
- **Context:** A hinge partner goes through a painful breakup with one secondary partner while remaining in a long-term marriage.
- **Dynamic:** Grief management within a continuing relationship.
- **What they want:** Allowing the grieving partner space to mourn a real romantic loss without making the continuing spouse feel inadequate.
- **Before:** Acknowledging that polyamorous breakups are real heartbreak that requires real mourning time.
- **Experience:** The spouse provides loving emotional space, making tea, listening without judgment, and resisting the urge to say 'I told you so' or celebrate the departure of a rival.
- **Branches:** If the grieving partner becomes emotionally distant for weeks, they schedule gentle re-connection dates to nurture the surviving bond.
- **Aftercare:** A weekend trip to nature, allowing the grieving partner to process the closure and recommit to the marriage.
- **Reflection:** Holding space for your partner's grief over someone else is the highest pinnacle of loving compersion.
- **Haven opportunities:** Poly breakup grief support guide; spouse caretaking boundaries; relationship reconnection roadmap.
